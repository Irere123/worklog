# Raise the ceiling: what Worklog takes from Katie Dill's talk

_Researched 2026-11-01. The talk is Katie Dill (Head of Design, Stripe), "Raise the ceiling: how to scale intent, quality, and artistry with AI," recorded at the Lenny and Friends Summit in San Francisco on 2026-09-10 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GLvFTMtw4Jk)). YouTube refused every download from this server and from the preview browser ("confirm you're not a bot"), so this note works from the official description and from timestamped write-ups that quote the talk: [The Podcast Summary](https://www.thepodcastsummary.com/episodes/GLvFTMtw4Jk/) (five articles), [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/d879ed36c7a067d8) and [John Ma's notes](https://johnmaartifacts.substack.com/p/ai-tip-1-design-for-the-metric-not). The quotes below carry the timestamps those sources give._

## The argument

After the Second World War, builders copied modernism faster than anyone could think about it: "the thinking got thinner and thinner, and all that was left were generic patterns ill suited to the context at hand" (1:01). The results were zombie buildings. AI is software's building boom, and without care it produces **zombie UI** (4:26). She watches for three things:

1. **Probable isn't original.** LLMs "are really good at telling you the most probable answer… less good at telling you what's original or specific to you, your brand, and your users' context" (2:15).
2. **The temptation of done.** A microwave burrito is ready in 90 seconds and nearly inedible, "but I'm so enamored by the speed of execution. The same happens with AI" (3:21).
3. **Disposability.** Easy work "often feels disposable and worst is treated that way… who's maintaining this" (3:54).

## Four practices

1. **Have a point of view.** "If you don't, AI will give it for you" (5:42). Stripe's chosen value is optimism. The quality bar doesn't move for generated work: "Why should that matter how it was made? It doesn't matter to the users" (7:22). In "Pepsi bubbling," the team goes "one level deeper than what your customer could see" (8:03). Taste is "getting really good at noticing" (8:21).
2. **Encode standards into the machine.** "The object of design is no longer the screen. It is the system itself" (9:38). Gutenberg cut 290 letterforms, not 52, so machine-set type could feel handmade: "both extensible and opinionated" (10:36). "The old system scaled consistency, but the new system needs to scale intent" (10:47). Stripe's design MCP gave three people three different results from one prompt (11:16), so they built a CLI on the design system that hands out whole flows and templates. One more line from the talk: "A system can satisfy every rule and still be dead."
3. **Refuse to confuse done with good** (12:23). Filtering used to happen before building; now it has to happen after, "when it is a lot harder to say no." An editor "takes accountability for every decision, which is in many ways every pixel" (14:46). One event animation took 56 iterations to get "a little bit of life" (15:49).
4. **Unleash creativity.** "The best practices have not been written yet." Give the model specific inputs, push its outputs with adversarial passes, invent interfaces beyond chat and CLIs, and protect the strange: spend the time AI saves on craft. Her examples of cared-for software include a calendar tab that shows the date. "AI can help us raise the ceiling, not just the floor."

## What changed in Worklog

| Practice | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Point of view | [docs/DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) says who Worklog is to people (honest, calm, human), how it sounds, its signature details and the editor's checklist. AGENTS.md points every agent at it. |
| Encode standards | `apps/web/scripts/design-check.ts` runs with `bun run lint` and in CI. It fails on hover outside `media.hover`, off-scale font weights, non-Phosphor icons, sparkles for AI, `transition: all`, and copy like "Something went wrong." Exceptions carry a reason (`// design-allow:`). It found 12 breaks on its first run. |
| Done is not good | Every screen was walked on a laptop and a 390px phone, in light and dark, with a screenshot script, and what it turned up was fixed. The pin stack covered list footers and the last row on every page, so it now docks in the sidebar. The Home brief preview dropped `#` from card numbers and cut words mid-way. Activity and card histories showed three rows for one person adding, moving and assigning a card; runs now fold into one line ("Added this card · Moved to Doing · Assigned to Atlas"), and a row whose body repeats its title says it once. An agent's page listed every session twice. Relative times disagreed ("5 hours ago" beside "4h"). Raw values leaked into the UI: "cursor", "skipped", "owner", "idle", "next tomorrow". The signed-in owner was labelled "Invited". People without a title were called "Teammate". Phones showed "⌘↵ sends", and other systems showed ⌘ where the key is Ctrl. Connections had letter tiles where real marks exist, plus an empty box restating the list below it. Brief figures were five boxed dashboard tiles; they're now a ruled strip, like a printed report. |
| Creativity | Agents get a face drawn from their id instead of six identical robot icons. The browser tab carries the inbox count, as a calendar's tab shows the date. Home opens with the day in one honest sentence written from the record ("4 things need you, including a decision for Atlas. Since yesterday: 3 cards closed and 18 cards added."). Activity opens with the last 24 hours as lanes, one per person or agent and a tick per event; a lane is a filter. A card's closure stamp is pressed when it has just happened, and live activity glows once as it arrives. The sign-in page is a logbook's ruled page instead of a blue glow, and says what Worklog is in one line. |
